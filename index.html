    RecipeSmoked Wings
    • Par-smoke, freeze, flash-fry.
    • 5 lb wings, split
    • 2 tsp baking powder per 2 lb (this is what crisps the skin — baking powder, not soda)
    • Salt, pepper, garlic powder, paprika
    • Dry the wings thoroughly, toss with baking powder and seasoning, rest uncovered in the fridge overnight if you can.

    Smoke: 250°F to an internal temp of about 150°F — roughly 45–60 minutes.

    Pull early. Cool completely, freeze on a sheet pan, then bag.

    Night of: thaw in the fridge, pat dry, fry at 375°F for 3–4 minutes until the skin blisters and they hit 175–180°F internal.

    Sauce after.

    Wings are forgiving on the high end — 180°F+ renders the fat and gets better, unlike breast meat.

  • RecipePizza Muffin Pan Night
    • Everyone builds their own, portions handle themselves, and the 2-year-old gets plain cheese.
    • Method 1 — Dough cups (best texture)
    • Pizza dough from the bread machine, divided into 12. Press each into a greased muffin cup and up the sides. Sauce, cheese, toppings. 425°F for 15–18 minutes.
    • Method 2 — Biscuit cups (fastest)
    • Canned biscuits, flattened and pressed into the cups. Same fill, 375°F for 15 minutes.
    • Method 3 — Bread cups

    Slice of sandwich bread, crusts off, rolled flat with a rolling pin, pressed in and buttered. 375°F for 12–15 minutes.

    Cheapest version, uses up bread.

    Notes: Go light on sauce. Overloaded cups turn soupy and won't hold together.

    Let them sit 5 minutes before lifting them out; they firm up as they cool.

    These freeze and reheat well at 350°F for 10 minutes — worth a double batch.

    Pizza roll variation: roll the dough flat, sauce and cheese it, roll it up like a cinnamon roll, slice into pinwheels, stand them in the muffin cups.

  • Compound Butter Bar
    RecipeCompound Butter Bar
    • Same method every time: 2 sticks softened butter, mix in, roll in parchment, twist the ends, freeze. Slice off coins as needed. All keep 3–4 months.
    • Cajun butter — 2 tbsp creole seasoning, 1 tsp garlic powder, 1 tsp paprika, squeeze of lemon → blackened fish, shrimp, corn on the cob, creole sausage and rice, baked potatoes
    • Lemon pepper butter — 2 tbsp lemon pepper, 1 tsp garlic powder, zest of 1 lemon → chicken tenders, wings, fish, green beans, the voodoo fry bowls
    • Steakhouse butter — 4 cloves garlic, 2 tbsp parsley, 1 tbsp Worcestershire, 1 tsp cracked pepper, ½ tsp salt → a coin melting on a steak as it rests, mashed potatoes, baked potatoes
    • Honey cinnamon butter — ¼ cup honey, 1 tsp cinnamon, pinch of salt → cornbread, biscuits, rolls, sweet potatoes, pancakes. The kids will eat this on anything.
    • Elote butter — 1 tbsp chili powder, ½ cup parmesan, zest of 1 lime, ½ tsp salt → corn on the cob, grilled corn, Mexican rice, quesadillas
    • Buffalo ranch butter — ¼ cup wing sauce,
    • 1 tbsp ranch seasoning → wings, chicken sandwiches, loaded tots, baked potatoes
    • Herb butter — ¼ cup mixed parsley, chives, thyme, 1 tsp salt, squeeze of lemon → pork tenderloin, fish, rolls, roasted vegetables, egg noodles
    • Bacon jalapeño butter — 6 slices crumbled bacon, ⅓ cup minced pickled jalapeño, 1 tsp garlic powder → cornbread, baked potatoes, burgers, cheesesteaks
    • Miso garlic butter — 2 tbsp white miso, 4 cloves garlic, 1 tsp sesame oil → ramen bar, rice bowls, green beans, corn, noodles
    • Making them
    • Soften the butter on the counter, don't melt it — melted butter won't hold the mix-ins evenly. Mash with a fork, not a mixer. Label the parchment; frozen logs all look identical.
    • Roll and freeze the log whole — firm butter slices clean, soft butter smears and loses its shape. A good sharp knife takes a coin off a frozen log with no trouble.
